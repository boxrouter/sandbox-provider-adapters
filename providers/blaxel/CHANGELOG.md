# Changelog

## 0.3.10

- 0.3.10: documentation only. The README states why `pause_resume` stays unclaimed (Blaxel's control-plane record does not show 5 to 8 percent of the standbys the sandbox performs), the measured pause and resume times, and the host's 5 s quiet window; the traces and sample logs are kept with the Blaxel evidence. No code change.

## 0.3.9

- 0.3.9: one read prices a sandbox. `limits.cost_final_within_seconds` is 120 (it was 900), and an ended sandbox's `cost` is `final` 120 s after its end once a bucket has a cost (it was 150 s, and any bucket counted, including Blaxel's 0 for an hour the sandbox did not run). A sandbox whose record Blaxel has dropped is `final` at once with the sum of its buckets, and `not_found` when no bucket has a cost: Blaxel keeps a deleted sandbox's record 16 minutes at least (often more than 50) and publishes its buckets within seconds, so nothing is inferred from bucket hours any more. Evidence, live through Cloud on 2026-11-23 (standard image, the benchmark's create): 40 sandboxes, lives of 3 to 150 s, 30 at once; every bucket was in the metrics 0 to 7 s after the destroy and the same at every later read (121 to 337 s after the end); of sixteen records two were dropped about 16 minutes after the destroy, nine were still there at 15 minutes and five at 52 minutes. With pricing's `after_s` and `final_after_s` both at 150 s, Cloud's first read is the final one: a burst of 30 through Cloud on this version was priced and final at that read for 29 (167 to 197 s after the destroy), and the live `contract.cost_final` row passed against the 120 s declaration (126 s). The 30th (a 30 s life) had no metrics row at all 13 minutes after its end, so its cost stays Unknown: Blaxel does not report every sandbox (1 of 70 in these runs). A 404 for a `cost` with the `created_at` hint is read as ended and dropped, so the hint is given only for a sandbox that has ended (Cloud's cost and image-copy passes; a capture gives none).

## 0.3.8

- 0.3.8: the `sandbox-api` that prepared images carry is pinned at 0.2.81 (it was 0.2.74: no `/standby` and no `/resume`, which first exist in 0.2.78; the only other path that differs, `/filesystem-copy`, is not used). And `BlaxelProvider.image_recipe_revision()`, new in SPS 0.5.77: 12 hex characters of a digest of the Dockerfile tail the adapter writes (both its forms), so it changes when the pinned `sandbox-api` image or version or the tail template changes, and only then; Cloud carries it in the identity of a prepared copy, so the copies prepared with 0.2.74 are not this copy and are prepared again by Cloud's own machinery. `pause_resume` stays withdrawn until the through-Cloud baseline has passed on a copy of the new recipe (Deploy 2).

## 0.3.7

- 0.3.7: `cost` of a sandbox whose record Blaxel has dropped (`GET /sandboxes/{name}` answers 404 and the caller gives `created_at`) becomes `final` once the hour of its newest bucket with a cost has closed and 150 s have passed: the sandbox has ended and its end is not known, so the end counted is that hour's close. Blaxel answers every hour of the window, with a cost of 0 for the hours the sandbox did not run, so only a bucket with a cost counts. Before, that figure was never final (the check looked for a bucket starting at the close of the newest hour), and pricing counts no figure that is not final, so every cost first read after Blaxel dropped the record stayed Unknown (full run 2026-11-23: 30 of 30 sampled such sandboxes read through this path answered `accruing` with the right total 3.5 h after their end; with this change all 30 are `final` with that total). The rule rests on Blaxel publishing a sandbox's buckets before it drops the record: live through Cloud on 2026-11-23, the bucket of a 3 s to 100 s sandbox was in the metrics within seconds of the destroy while the record still answered `TERMINATED` more than 100 s later. A sandbox read while Blaxel still holds its record is unchanged (final 150 s after its `updatedAt`, once a bucket's hour holds that end; an end exactly on the hour is held by the bucket that closes there). A dropped record with no bucket that has a cost stays `accruing`.

## 0.3.5

- 0.3.5: the `pause_resume` claim is withdrawn (it was in 0.3.4, for a day). `POST /standby` and `POST /resume` are in the sandbox's own `sandbox-api` from 0.2.78 on; the images this adapter prepares carried 0.2.74, so every pause of a Cloud-created sandbox answered 404 in production (full run 2026-11-23). `pause` and `resume` stay in the package; discovery and the snapshots no longer claim the section and `create` refuses it. A 404 from either call reads the sandbox record (failure path only); before, it surfaced as a provider error with unknown outcome. A sandbox that has ended is `not_found`. For a sandbox that is there, `/standby` did nothing and is `unsupported` (failed, "prepare the image again"), while `/resume` is a success: Blaxel's host wakes a sandbox on the request before the old sandbox-api answers 404, so the sandbox runs (this also reaches a sandbox that went to idle standby by itself; `/resume` is absent in sandbox-api 0.2.74 as well). The `sandbox-api` pin of the prepared images (`dockerfile.py`, 0.2.74) is NOT changed here. It returns once the images carry a `sandbox-api` that has the calls (the pin is `dockerfile.py`) and the conformance row has passed on one of them, through Cloud.
- 0.3.4: `pause` and `resume` (`pause_resume`, `keeps: files_and_memory`), Blaxel's standby asked for. `pause` is `POST /standby?force=true` on the sandbox's own host (202: accepted, not done; `force` puts it in standby although a keep-alive process holds it awake, and Blaxel takes the hold back at the resume) and then reads of the sandbox record until its `state` is `STANDBY` (`status` stays `DEPLOYED`): a read after 0.5 s (a standby is never there before 2 s), then after pauses doubling to 1 s, for at most 60 s (`timeout`, outcome unknown, if Blaxel never reports it). The request is sent once more, past the host's quiet window, if no standby is seen 8 s after it (live: a request to a sandbox already in standby woke it, and one holding a keep-alive process stayed running; a request in a young sandbox's first seconds is ignored): that is the variant `pause[resend]`. After the resend only a STANDBY whose `lastUsedAt` is at or after the resend's own `Date` counts: the resend wakes a sandbox that had already gone to standby while the record still said RUNNING, and the first request's STANDBY then shows in the record while the guest runs. Any failure of a read after the 202 (a 401, 403, 429, 5xx) is outcome `unknown`, since the standby was accepted; a caller-fault code becomes `provider_error`. 501 is `unsupported` for the standby call only. `lifetime_extend` answers `running` after a resume the same way `get` does. Live 2026-11-23 the record said STANDBY 2.8 to 5.5 s after the request, except when the host ignored it (its documented quiet window at the start of a sandbox's life: 2 of 4 sandboxes, paused at 1, 4, 8 and 11 s of age): such a sandbox went to standby on its own about 15 s after its last request, 18 to 22 s after the call. A STANDBY record that Blaxel dates before the request (its `lastUsedAt` is when the standby began) is the record of an earlier standby that a resume has not yet cleared, so a pause right after a resume waits for its own standby. `resume` is `POST /resume` on the same host, one exchange, 0.1 s live; the next command ran 0.1 s later. The guest is frozen, not restarted: the same PIDs, files and memory, and the clock catches up. Blaxel's `state` still says STANDBY 2.7 to 5.4 s after a resume, so `get` and `list` of the object that sent the resume read a STANDBY record dated before the resume as `running`; a later standby, and another object's read, are reported as Blaxel states them. `lifetime_timer.counts_paused_time` stays `true`: the ttl counts from creation (`expiresIn` fell during a standby). `create` meets `pause_resume.keeps` of `files_and_memory`. `wire-budget.json`: `pause` 1 (the POST; each pause of the `poll` schedule, 0.5 s doubling to 1 s, is followed by a read) with `lookup` 1, `pause[resend]` 2; `resume` 1 with `lookup` 1. The discovery snapshots now carry `pause_resume` and the hour-long `run_timeout_ms` of 0.3.2.
- 0.3.3: `wire-budget.json` only, no adapter change: `lifetime_extend` is `{"exchanges": 2}`,
  `GET /sandboxes/{name}` (the ttl and `expiresIn`) and, only when the ttl must grow,
  `PUT /sandboxes/{name}` with the new ttl; a ttl that already covers the seconds asked is the read
  alone (1). Neither is a lookup. Cloud sends it after every resume on a class that offers
  `lifetime.extend` (cloud#9580) and on `POST /lifetime`; with no key the benchmark's exchange audit
  withholds those steps. Blaxel's `pause` and `resume` budgets land separately; the resume budget
  and this key must agree then (a resume request is `resume` plus `lifetime.extend`).
- 0.3.2: a command is one waited `POST /process` whatever its timeout, as Blaxel's SDK sends it, and `limits.run_timeout_ms` is an hour (it was 600 s). With 600 s, Core's 600 s command (610 s with its kill margin) went past the limit, so Core started a process and polled its output: a process start, then a process read and a logs read a pass with pauses up to 2 s (seven exchanges at the median of the 2026-11-22 benchmark runs, about 59 ms over the SDK). A timeout over 90 s no longer starts the command detached: the waited request carries the whole timeout as Blaxel's own `timeout`, which still ends the command and every process it started at its deadline. Blaxel's gateway cuts a waited request near 120 s (504) while the named command runs on: only then is the command read by its name (at once, then 0.05 s doubling to 0.5 s) and its logs read once, as the detached path did; the POST is never sent twice. A command Blaxel does not know after the cut was never started (`timeout`, outcome unknown), and one found `killed` well before its deadline is a `provider_error`, never a timeout. `wire-budget.json`: `run[detached]` becomes `run[cut]` (same exchanges and poll); `run` is 1 for every timeout up to an hour.
- 0.3.2 (live-checked: Blaxel keeps the command running after the gateway's 120 s 504). The follow after the cut backs off from 0.05 s to 2 s, and to 5 s once the command has run 300 s (it capped at 0.5 s: two reads a second; a 150 s command cost 56 reads), and a pause never passes the deadline: at most about 1.7% of the run time late, 0.5 then 0.2 reads a second. The output comes from the process record (`GET /process/{name}` carries `stdout` and `stderr`, running or ended, equal to the logs read live), so `process.output` is one exchange a pass (it was two), and a timed-out or cut `run` reads no logs. The logs are read only when the record may not hold all of it: a stream missing or not text, a stream starting with Blaxel's `[... truncated` marker, a stream shorter than the cursor, or an ended process with no output at all. `wire-budget.json`: `process_output` 1 (no `per_poll`), `run[timeout]` 2, `run[cut]` 2 with `cap_s` 2.
- The one read that only finds a handle is marked as a lookup (`sandbox_provider_spec.v1.timing.lookup`), so Cloud counts it in `provider_calls` but does not publish its time as the operation's: the sandbox record read that learns its runtime address (once per sandbox per provider object, in `_base`). Nothing else is marked: not the workspace list or account read (account open), the cwd read, the user checks, the create poll, the image reads or cost. Exchanges per operation are unchanged; `tests/test_provider_calls.py` pins which exchange is a lookup (cold and warm).
- `run` with `output: base64` (or a `user`) is cheaper inside the sandbox: a short `sh` script (not `bash`, about 1 KB, not 2.4 KB) writes the two streams to `<stage>.o` and `<stage>.e` (no staging directory), and one `wc`, `head`, `base64` and `rm` give the answer (it was a `mkdir`, two each of `wc`, `head`, `base64`, `tr`, and an `rm`). The first `min(output_limit_bytes, 32 KiB)` bytes of each stream come back in one base64 text; a stream over 32 KiB that the limit allows more of is still read from its file. The exact bytes, the exit status and the errors are unchanged (tested under dash and BusyBox `sh`). A timed-out wrapper no longer reads the process and logs (the next entry).
- Builds its HTTP clients with `sandbox_provider_spec.v1.connections.provider_client`, which keeps an idle connection for 300 s (up to 500 idle, 1,000 open) instead of httpx's 5 s and 20: a call that comes back to a host after a short gap, often on another API process, no longer pays a new TCP and TLS handshake (about two round trips). Exchanges per operation do not change; connections do.
- `files.list` is Blaxel's own `GET /filesystem/{dir}` (one call), not a `python3` program run through a process: a file's size and its type (from the listing's mode, an octal Go `FileMode`: file, symlink or other) come from the listing, and a page that has a directory adds one `stat` for those directories' sizes (a directory has no size in the listing). `.` and `..` path segments are normalized before the call (Blaxel refuses `..` on the wire; a `..` that climbs out of the path resolves against the sandbox's working directory, which one `pwd` command learns once — the working directory depends on the image and the user). Order, paging and the `not_found` and `invalid_request` errors are the same.
- `files.write` with a `user` is two calls, not four: the staging upload, then one command that checks the user exists (ending 126, `not_found`), copies as the user and removes the staging file. Before, a `getent` call came first and a separate `rm` last.
- A byte-exact `run` that times out is 4 calls, not 6, and an adapter command that times out is 1: the wrapper's answer is the staging files, so the process record and logs reads after Blaxel's 422 are gone (they were discarded).
- `process.output` with a wait reads again after a pause that doubles from 0.1 s to 2 s (it paused a fixed 0.2 s, two calls a pass: a 60 s wait was about 600 calls, now about 70). `scripts/core/check_no_sleeps.py` now fails a fixed-interval poll in any package.

## 0.3.0

- Claimed after the live tester passed them (SPS 0.5.43): `user_selection` (commands, files_write,
  process_start), private previews (credential `link` or `header`, revocable) beside public ones,
  `processes` `reattach`, and `previews` `max_ports`. A prepared image is ready only once Blaxel
  lists its tag, each build carries a label with its own name (Blaxel reuses an identical build and
  then names no tag), and deleting an image that a sandbox still uses is accepted and swept by the
  next `image.prepare`. `hidden_secrets` (Blaxel's proxy routes: sets a header, all HTTPS) is claimed too, after `hidden_secrets.injected` and `.unreadable` passed against the receiver fixtures.
  No call that changes something is sent twice inside the adapter.

- The 1.0 additions that Blaxel serves, each claimed in discovery after the live tester passes it:
  - `user` on `run`, `process.start` and `files.write` (`user_selection`): a command runs through a
    `setpriv`-or-`su` switch after the adapter has found that the user exists; a file is copied
    into place by the user, so they own it and are refused where they may not write.
  - `run` with `output: base64` (`run.base64`): exact bytes through a wrapper that keeps each
    stream in a file and reads it back as base64 (or from the file above 32 KiB).
  - Prepared images (`image.prepare`, `image.get`, `image.list`, `image.delete`, `custom_images`
    `prepared` for `oci` and `dockerfile` with a build context): a Blaxel upload build whose
    sandbox is kept as the image's record, which holds its labels and request fingerprint. A failed
    build carries `failed_step` when a command of the customer's own Dockerfile failed.
  - Private, expiring and revocable previews (`preview.revoke`, `previews` `access`, `credential:
    header`, `revocable`).
  - `labels.update`, `Sandbox.times` (from the record's `events[]`), `secrets` on create
    (`hidden_secrets`, Blaxel's proxy routes), `account.cost` and the `created_at` hint of `cost`.

## 0.2.0

- The optional 1.0 sections that Blaxel serves: `process.write_stdin`, `process.close_stdin` and
  `process.cancel` (`processes` with `input` and `cancel`), `preview.link` (`previews`, public
  only, port 8080 reserved), `terminal.open` (`terminal`, through a launcher script that records
  the exit status), `files.list`, `files.mkdir`, `files.remove` and `files.exists`, and
  `lifetime.extend`. Each is claimed in discovery only after the live tester passes it.
- `create` accepts the `processes`, `previews` and `terminal` requirements.
- Depends on `websockets`.
- Not ported: Blaxel's archive and its snapshots, by decision (its standby is `pause_resume` since 0.3.4).
